## 11. Limitations

No evaluation study is clean end-to-end. This section states what constrains the Phase 1 results and what we would fix before calling any number “final” for Scale-C deployment.

### 11.1 Scope: Tier 1 MCQ only

The report’s main question is which model Scale-C should use for H5P-based teaching. Phase 1 only measures **multiple-choice accuracy** on pooled public benchmarks (Scale-C Tier 1). We did **not** evaluate:

- valid **H5P JSON** or structured activity generation (Tier 2);
- **didactic quality** of explanations or distractors;
- **German** or multilingual content (Tier 3);
- **safety** refusals on exploit-style or misuse prompts.

Any shortlist in §10 is therefore based on a **proxy task**. A model that wins on MCQ may still fail when Scale-C asks for schema-conformant H5P—that is the largest gap between this report and a production decision.

### 11.2 Incomplete and uneven runs

Several runs in `data/eval_1/` are not fully comparable:

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Llama 3.3 70B NLI partial** (327 of 573 scorable items) | NLI accuracy and Trendyol Δ (+9.4 pp) are provisional. |
| **Llama 3.3 embedding n = 1,353** vs **847** for other models | Embedding leaderboard and Trendyol Δ (−7.2 pp) use different denominators; includes non-MCQ task types in the same corpus. |
| **Mixed backends** (Hugging Face local, OpenAI-compatible API for Baron/Trendyol/Qwen3) | Latency and quantization (e.g. Q4 GGUF) may affect answer extraction differently than a single hosted stack. |

Before thesis defense, we should finish the 70B NLI run and harmonize embedding item sets—or split results into “strict MCQ” and “extended corpus” tables.

### 11.3 Topic labels are automatic, not gold

Items are assigned to Scale-C topics with **two zero-shot pipelines** (NLI and embedding) [9], [10]. We do not have human-verified topic gold labels at scale. NLI and embedding builds produce **different item pools** (573 vs 847 scorable under Phase 1), so classifier views are related but not identical experiments.

Topic- and course-level percentages inherit label noise. Large deltas on small topics (n &lt; 10) should be read as hints, not facts. Proposed taxonomy fields in `schema/taxonomy.json` (risk, difficulty, cognitive skill, tier) are **not validated** and were not used in these results.

### 11.4 Benchmark pool vs Scale-C course content

The evaluation set is a **merge of external benchmarks** (MMLU, CyberBench, CyberMetric, CyberSOCEval, SEC-bench, Global-MMLU, etc.) [11], capped at roughly **10 items per topic** in `data/final_1`. It overlaps Scale-C curriculum themes but is **not** the live H5P question bank. Models may be tuned to phrasing common in those sources rather than to Scale-C’s classroom wording.

### 11.5 No statistical testing or error analysis

We report accuracy and percentage-point deltas without confidence intervals or significance tests. Topic slices with low support can swing by tens of points from a handful of items.

We also did **not** complete a qualitative error analysis (representative prompts, gold vs model answer, failure mode) for malware and advanced-threat items. §8 identifies those areas as weak; we cannot yet say whether failures are due to knowledge gaps, ambiguous wording, bad distractors, or answer-parsing bugs.

### 11.6 Pairwise design limits

Comparisons are **within family** (base vs fine-tuned), not a full factorial across all training recipes. We cannot isolate fine-tuning objective, data size, or LoRA rank from model card descriptions alone [7]. Cross-family rankings (e.g. Lily vs Llama 3.1) confound architecture, tokenizer, chat template, and scale.

## 12. Conclusion

Scale-C needs a language model that is technically sound on security content, usable for H5P authoring, and realistic to host at a university. This report documents **Phase 1** of that search: a pairwise benchmarking study over six base/fine-tuned families, two topic-labeling views, and dozens of fine-grained curriculum topics drawn from public cyber benchmarks.

### 12.1 What we learned

**Cyber fine-tuning is not a free upgrade.** Lily and Baron improve over their bases on Tier 1 MCQ; Foundation-Sec and ZySec regress; Trendyol’s benefit depends on how topics are labeled. For Scale-C, the practical rule is simple: **compare against the base you would actually deploy**, and do not trust marketing labels alone.

**Overall scores are not enough.** Identity, network, and certificate-style material separates models less than malware, advanced threats, and broad theory. Scale-C should assume **human review** for the hard modules no matter which LLM we shortlist.

**Measurement choices matter.** NLI and embedding mostly agree on who is weak and who is strong, but they disagree on the 70B fine-tuning story and on several topic-level deltas. A single automatic pipeline is not sufficient for a final model pick.

**Generative models are the only serious candidates.** Encoder adaptation (CyBERTuned) does not close the gap to a small instruct LLM for MCQ—and Scale-C’s real needs (generation, structure, locale) sit even further from encoder ranking.

### 12.2 Recommendation for Scale-C (provisional)

We **cannot** name Scale-C’s production model from Phase 1 alone. We **can** narrow the field:

| Role | Model | Status |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Primary Tier 2 candidates** | Lily-Cybersecurity-7B; Llama 3.1 8B (base) | Run H5P/JSONSchemaBench and rubric-scored explanations next. |
| **High-capacity option (pending)** | Llama 3.3 70B Instruct vs Trendyol Cyber 70B | Complete same-item re-evaluation; then cost/hosting review. |
| **Fallback / conditional** | BaronLLM v2 | Only if Tier 2 shows strength after Qwen3 serving is verified. |
| **Avoid for Tier 1 MCQ** | Foundation-Sec-8B; ZySec-7B; encoder-only paths | Bases outperform or architecture unsuitable. |

Until Tier 2 and Tier 3 run, the defensible thesis statement is: **we have a evidence-based shortlist and avoid list for cyber MCQ, not a final Scale-C deployment choice.**

### 12.3 Contribution of this study

For the Scale-C project, this work provides a **reusable evaluation pipeline**—taxonomy, multi-source dataset builder, dual topic views, pairwise reporting—and an honest first pass at model selection constraints.

For readers outside the project, the takeaway is methodological: **cyber LLM benchmarks that publish one accuracy number can overstate fine-tuning wins and hide topic-level losses.** Pairwise, multi-view, topic-labeled evaluation is slower to report but harder to misread.

### 12.4 Future work (ordered for Scale-C)

1. **Tier 2 — H5P and structured output:** JSONSchemaBench, H5P-oriented prompts, schema validation, human or rubric scoring.  
2. **Frozen 70B re-run:** full NLI corpus, matched embedding MCQ subset for Llama 3.3 vs Trendyol.  
3. **Safety evaluation:** refusal and safe-completion checks on misuse-related prompts.  
4. **Tier 3 — German / localization:** Global-MMLU and superGLEBer where the course requires DE content.  
5. **Error analysis appendix:** qualitative failures on malware/threat items.  
6. **Taxonomy validation:** human audit of topic labels; optional use of risk/difficulty metadata once validated.

When those steps are done, Scale-C will be in a position to commit to a model—or to a small ensemble (e.g. 7B for drafting, larger model for review)—with evidence that matches how the platform will actually use it.
